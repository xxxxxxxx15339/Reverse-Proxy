Context Cancellation is the mechanism Go uses to stop work that is no longer needed. It is a signal that travels down from the person who asked fo the work (the Client) to the person doing the work (the Server/Database), saying, "Stop everything, I don't care about the result anymore."


  1. The Analogy: Ordering Food
    Imagine you are at a restaurant
        * Request: You order a steak (The HTTP Request).
        * Processing: The waiter tells the chef. The chef puts a steak on the grill.
        * Cancellation: Suddenly, you get an emergency phone call and have to leave immediately. You tell the waiter, "I'm leaving, cancel the order."
        * Propagation: The waiter runs to the kitchen and tells the chef, "Stop cooking that steak!"
        * Result: The chef turns off the gas and saves the steak for someone else

    ==> Without Context Cancellation: You leave the restaurant. The waiter ignores you. The chef cooks the steak for 20 minutes. The waiter brings the steak to an empty table.
        * Result: Wasted gas, wasted food, wasted time. In a server, this is wasted memory and CPU.

  2. Why is it Critical for a Proxy?
    A Reverse Proxy sits in the middle. It is vulnerable to "hanging" connections.
        * User Browser connects to Your Proxy.
        * Your Proxy connects to Backend API.
        * The Scenario: The Backend is slow (taking 10 seconds). The User gets bored after 2 seconds and closes the tab.
        * The Problem: If you don't use Context, your Proxy stays connected to the Backend for 8 more seconds, waiting for a response that no one will ever read. If this happens 1000 times, your Proxy runs out of open ports and crashes.
